1 — The waterline which terms a fragment gets, by where the eye and the fragment sit

Everything the water does to a surface is decided by one segment: the part of the camera→fragment ray that is under the line. WaterPathLength() returns its length; zero means the water never touches that pixel.

2 — The shore one wave, from shoaling to dry sand

Cross-section, deep water on the left. Each labelled band is a separate knob in Foam › Shore & Swash. They fire in this order as the front travels, and they are additive — which is why the near-shore foam saturates before anything else does.

The trap on this picture. surfFoamGain, surfWaterlineFoam and surfSwashDepositGain control what is written into the foam buffer. The plunging-lip spray does not read those — it reads whether the surf layer is active at all. Zero every gain on this diagram and the white shapes vanish while the lip keeps throwing particles.

3 — The foam patch one saturating buffer, four writers, three readers

A foam patch is a value in one texture. Everything on the left adds into the same scalar and it clips at 1 — which is why shore whitewash and turbulence foam appear to fight: they are not two layers, they are one number.

Why foam looks right with Foam OFF. Turning Foam off does not remove a layer — it swaps the render path. With the write mask at 0 only the analytic whitewash survives, and that one was never competing for headroom. Turning Foam on doesn't fight the shore engine; it re-routes through a buffer the shore engine has already filled.
