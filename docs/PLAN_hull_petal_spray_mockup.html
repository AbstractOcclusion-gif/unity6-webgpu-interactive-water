Fit Spray To Hull — user POV

Mockup only. What the person actually sees, and where they see it.

Why a dedicated view is not optional. The water surface renders at the rest plane with ZWrite On. In the Scene view the submerged half of the hull — the exact half you are trying to slice — is behind an opaque sheet. You would be picking a draft line you cannot see.

A — Picking the draft side elevation, water suppressed

Nothing is written to the component until Apply. Everything before that is preview geometry.

B — Checking the flower top view, same tool, one chip click

C — The whole path from a boat prefab to spray on screen

  1. Add Component → Water Spray Pump. The inspector opens on an empty probe array with one line: "No probes. Fit to hull ›"
  2. Click Fit to hull. The view opens already showing the boat from the side, water suppressed, the draft line sitting on the body's rest plane, and a preview ring of probes.
  3. Drag the draft handle until the line sits where the boat really floats. The outline and probes re-slice as you drag. Or press Solve from buoyancy and skip this.
  4. Click the top chip to check the flower — probe spacing, arc width, how hard the petals rake.
  5. Apply to pump. Only now is anything serialized, in one Undo step.
  6. Enter play mode and drive. Petals swing astern as the boat accelerates.
The draft is not a number anyone knows. Nobody can tell you their boat floats at -0.18 m; they know what it looks like sitting in the water. So the default has to be right without being touched, and WaterBuoyancy already holds what is needed to compute it — float strength and the sphere-cap submerged fraction. Equilibrium is where buoyancy × submerged fraction = 1. That is what Solve from buoyancy does, and it means step 3 is usually skipped entirely.
